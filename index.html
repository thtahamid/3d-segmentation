01

SEER — Skill-Evolving Grounded Reasoning

Free-text interpretable segmentation. Translates unstructured clinical language into 3D masks via vision-language reasoning with a dynamically evolving skill bank.

Facts

ReleasedMar 2026
InstitutionFudan University
VLMQwen3-VL-4B-Instruct
Dice53.83 (BrainMet); 97.39 (PENGWIN)
Paper → Project →

Strengths

  • Robust to linguistic variability (81.94% variance reduction)
  • Worst-case Dice improved by 18.60%
  • Transferable across backbones
  • SEER self-refines through skill bank

Limitations

  • Requires GRPO training infrastructure
  • Limited to CT volumes in evaluation
  • Dependency on frozen segmentation backbone
  • Smaller training dataset (SEER-Trace ~22K)
vs. MicroSAM

Where MicroSAM segments from point/box prompts via a SAM encoder fine-tuned on 3D volumes, SEER operates entirely on free-text. No spatial prompt is needed — the model disambiguates language and generates a canonical canonical spec. This is a different interaction paradigm rather than a feature upgrade. MicroSAM remains better when you already have precise spatial prompts.

02

MedVL-SAM2 — Unified 3D VLM

One model. Report generation, VQA, semantic segmentation, referring segmentation, and interactive segmentation. Integrates SAM2's [SEG] token into a LLaVA-style 3D vision-language backbone.

Facts

ReleasedJan 2026
InstitutionUniversity of Florida
LLMInternVL-2.5-4B
TasksReport gen, VQA, segmentation
Paper →

Strengths

  • Single model for multiple clinical tasks
  • Multi-modal prompts (text, point, box)
  • Strong volumetric consistency via SAM2 memory
  • SOTA on CT-RATE VQA (89.74% MCQ)

Limitations

  • 4x token expansion vs 2D LLaVA (efficiency cost)
  • Multi-stage training pipeline is complex
  • SAM2 output unidirectional (no feedback to LLM)
  • Limited to CT data in current training
vs. MicroSAM

MedVL-SAM2 is not a drop-in replacement for MicroSAM — it is a completely different architecture designed for multi-task reasoning. Where MicroSAM excels at pure segmentation from spatial prompts with a lightweight footprint, MedVL-SAM2 adds report generation, VQA, and referring segmentation at the cost of a larger model (4B LLM backbone + SAM2 decoder). If your need is pure segmentation, MicroSAM is simpler; if you need dialog and grounding, MedVL-SAM2 covers a broader clinical workflow.

03

BiSegMamba — Bidirectional Tri-Oriented Mamba

Pure Mamba architecture for 3D medical segmentation with 77.9% fewer FLOPs than SegMamba-V2. Bidirectional orthogonal scanning replaces both Transformer attention and CNN locality biases.

Facts

ReleasedMay 2026
InstitutionBeihang University
BackboneState Space Model (Mamba)
Dice91.63 (BraTS); 92.57 (ACDC)
Paper → GitHub →

Strengths

  • 77.9% fewer FLOPs than SegMamba-V2
  • Bidirectional scan removes causal bias
  • Adaptive directional fusion learns scan weights
  • Anisotropic-aware (depth-preserving)
  • Good for elongated/vascular structures

Limitations

  • No language interface (no prompts, text, etc.)
  • Fixed to segmentation task only
  • Sequence length may limit large volumes
  • Less community adoption than SAM-family
vs. MicroSAM

BiSegMamba uses a pure Mamba state-space backbone whereas MicroSAM builds on a fine-tuned SAM visual encoder. BiSegMamba is ~3x lighter in FLOPs and avoids the transformer's quadratic cost entirely. However, it is segmentation-only — no promptability, no text, no interactive refinement. BiSegMamba is a strong backbone if you need fast volumetric segmentation; MicroSAM is closer to an interactive foundation model.

04

SegviGen — Repurposing Generative Models for Part Segmentation

Turns a pretrained 3D color generative model into a part segmenter, using part-indicative colorization with only 0.32% of labeled training data.

Facts

ReleasedMar 2026
InstitutionBeihang University
BackbonePretrained 3D generative prior
Key gain40% improvement (interactive)
Paper → Project →

Strengths

  • Extremely data-efficient (0.32% labels)
  • Natively supports interactive and full modes
  • Optional 2D guidance for refinement
  • Part segmentation without part vocabularies

Limitations

  • Requires pretrained 3D generative model
  • Not designed for medical images (natural objects)
  • Single prompt type (point-based)
  • Colorization adds inference overhead
vs. MicroSAM

SegviGen and MicroSAM have opposite strengths. MicroSAM is medical-first, point/box promptable, and segment-anything styled. SegviGen is built for general 3D assets (not medical) and relies on generative-model priors for part segmentation. MicroSAM is the more suitable choice for medical images; SegviGen may inspire future medical adaptations.

05

VoxTell — Free-Text Promptable Universal 3D Segmentation

Vision-language model trained on 62K+ CT/MRI/PET volumes and 1K+ anatomical classes that maps free-form text descriptions directly to 3D masks.

Facts

ReleasedNov 2025
InstitutionMIC-DKFZ
Data62K+ volumes, 1K+ classes
ModalitiesCT, MRI, PET
GitHub →

Strengths

  • Cross-modality (CT + MRI + PET)
  • Zero-shot generalization to unseen classes
  • Free-text both single-word and full clinical sentences
  • Robust to linguistic variation

Limitations

  • Not interactive (no point/box refinement in free-text mode)
  • Without prompts, accuracy drops
  • Large model with steep GPU requirements
  • No support for video or 4D data
vs. MicroSAM

VoxTell extends free-text prompting beyond what MicroSAM offers — it works across CT, MRI, and PET, supports 1K+ classes zero-shot, and handles rich clinical language. MicroSAM is purely spatial-prompt-based (points/boxes) and limited to the classes or volumes it was fine-tuned on. Choose VoxTell for open-vocabulary universality; MicroSAM for precise spatial prompting with lighter compute.

06

FDIF — Formula-Driven Implicit Functions

Pre-trains 3D segmentation networks with zero real medical images or expert annotations. Synthesizes geometry and texture using signed distance function (SDF) formulas.

Facts

ReleasedApr 2026
InstitutionMultiple (Kitasato, RIKEN, etc.)
Pre-training dataZero real medical images
RepresentationSDFs (implicit)
Paper → GitHub →

Strengths

  • No real data or expert annot needed for pre-training
  • Matches self-supervised methods pre-trained on large real datasets
  • Generalizes to classification as well
  • Surface-voxel representational richness over polygon meshes

Limitations

  • Still needs some labeled real data for downstream fine-tuning
  • Controllable synthesis requires careful formula design
  • Slower to synthesize diverse textures than real data pre-training
  • Not interactive or promptable
vs. MicroSAM

FDIF is a complementary paradigm, not a competitor. It shows that you can pre-train a segmentation network — potentially even the SAM backbone MicroSAM relies on — without ever touching real patient data. This is valuable for privacy-sensitive settings. MicroSAM uses real medical annotated data from the start; FDIF opens the door to pre-training on purely synthetic supervision.

07

MedSAM3 — Segment Anything With Medical Concepts

The latest generation of the MedSAM family that takes SAM-style volumetric segmentation deeper into medical concept understanding.

Facts

ReleasedNov 2025
AuthorsA. Liu, R. Xue, X.R. Cao et al.
BackboneSAM / SAM2 family
Key FeatureMedical concept integration
Paper → GitHub →

Strengths

  • Continues MedSAM lineage (strong baseline)
  • Supports point/box prompts like SAM
  • Adds medical concept understanding to mask decoder
  • Interoperable with SAM-family tooling

Limitations

  • No multi-task (text/VQA/segmentation) support
  • Less publicly documented than MedSAM2
  • Still limited to SAM encoder architecture
  • Slight improvements may not justify migration
vs. MicroSAM

MedSAM3 and MicroSAM sit in the same prompt-based segmentation lineage. MedSAM3 adds medical concept embedding layers between the SAM encoder and decoder. MicroSAM is more of a direct fine-tune of SAM on 3D medical data. MedSAM3 is expected to outperform MicroSAM slightly on concept-poor or rare anatomy due to its added domain calibration, but they are architecturally very similar.

08

P3-SAM — Native 3D Part Segmentation

Native 3D point-prompted part segmentation model for any 3D objects, trained on a dataset of ~3.7 million models. Not medical-specific.

Facts

ReleasedSep 2025
ProviderTencent Hunyuan
Data~3.7M 3D models
Task3D part segmentation
Paper → Project →

Strengths

  • Massive training data scale for 3D understanding
  • Any 3D object compatible (not limited to medical)
  • Native 3D operation (not slice-by-slice)
  • Robust across complex poly objects

Limitations

  • Not trained or validated on medical data
  • No point-cloud medical modality support (CT/MRI)
  • Limited information on benchmarks
  • Chinese-only documentation as of release
vs. MicroSAM

P3-SAM is best understood as a non-medical cousin of the MicroSAM approach: point-prompted, 3D-native segmentation. Neither model is useful on the other's domain without fine-tuning. MicroSAM wins for CT/MRI medical imaging; P3-SAM wins for generic 3D assets.

Where MicroSAM stands today

MicroSAM remains strong. The question for your project is not "which is best" but "which problem are you solving now."

MicroSAM strengths

Point and box promptable, fine-tuned on real CT/MRI volumes, interactive through SAM's proven interface, battle-tested across dozens of downstream tasks.

Where newer models win

Free-text prompting (SEER, VoxTell, MedVL-SAM2), multi-task unification (MedVL-SAM2), data-free pre-training (FDIF), lighter compute (BiSegMamba), or broader zero-shot coverage (VoxTell).

Stay with MicroSAM when

You need precise spatial prompting, want to minimize dependencies, operate on single-modality medical volumes, and prefer a pipeline that is well-documented and community-supported.

Migrate when

You need free-text input, interactive reasoning and answer generation alongside masks, lower compute for batch processing, or zero-shot across CT/MRI/PET without retraining.
