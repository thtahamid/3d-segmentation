📋 What this report answers

The main survey (survey_report.html) covers the 3D segmentation landscape broadly. This companion goes deep on one specific, frequently-asked question: how does micro-SAM — the dominant open-source Segment-Anything tool for microscopy — actually compare to Meta's newer video/3D SAM family (SAM2, SAM2.1, SAM3, and the brand-new SAM 3D Objects/Body released November 2025)? And separately: what genuinely new 3D segmentation models has 2025–2026 produced that the original survey didn't cover, especially in the medical imaging field?

🔑 Headline finding

No paper directly benchmarks micro-SAM against SAM2 on the same microscopy dataset with the same metric. The two projects grew in separate communities — micro-SAM in bio-image analysis, SAM2 in clinical/video imaging. What does exist is each family benchmarked separately against shared baselines (nnU-Net, MitoNet, Cellpose, VISTA3D) — plus one very current, verified fact: micro-SAM's own research group now directly benchmarks μSAM against SAM2 and SAM3 in a March 2026 paper, even though the core micro-SAM library itself still ships only a SAM1 backbone as of its latest release (v1.8.4, confirmed via PyPI dependencies). See the Head-to-Head tab for the verified detail — and a correction of an earlier, mistaken "SAM2 backbone merge" claim this report initially picked up from a research pass that turned out to be unreliable.

🔵 Meta's Segment-Anything Video & 3D Family

SAM2's core trick — treat a sequence as a stream of frames connected by a memory bank — turns out to generalize surprisingly well to volumetric slices. This section covers SAM2 itself, its medical fine-tune MedSAM2, the zero-shot "slice-as-video" adaptation SAM2Point, SAM3, and Meta's all-new (Nov 2025) SAM 3D reconstruction models, which are commonly confused with 3D segmentation but solve a different problem.

🧭 Animated Explorer: How SAM2's Memory-Attention Pipeline Works

Watch the flowing dashes: each new frame/slice is encoded once, cross-attends to a bounded memory bank of recent frames, and the mask decoder's occlusion head can flag "object not visible here" — the mechanism medical/EM papers repurpose to propagate a mask across a 3D volume.

This bounded ~6-frame memory window is precisely why long volumes can drift ("error accumulation," see SAM2Long, arXiv:2410.16268) — the model gradually loses context from far-away slices.

SAM2 / SAM 2.1 ↗

Meta

Unified image+video promptable segmentation. Hiera hierarchical ViT encoder (MAE-pretrained) + memory attention + FIFO memory bank (~6 recent frames + prompted frame + object pointers) + mask decoder with an added occlusion-prediction head. SAM1 was image-only and re-encoded per prompt; SAM2 encodes once per frame and streams.

SA-V dataset: 50.9K videos, 642.6K masklets, ~35.5M masks. Benchmarks: DAVIS17 val J&F ≈90.7 (+2.6 vs prior SOTA); MOSE val J&F ≈77.9 (+6.2, new SOTA); needs 3× fewer clicks than prior SAM+tracker baselines; image 1-click mIoU 58.9 vs SAM1's 58.1 at 6× the speed.

MedSAM2 ↗

Medical

BoWang Lab's fine-tune of SAM2.1 (base: sam2.1_hiera_tiny) for 3D medical volumes and clinical video (ultrasound/endoscopy). Workflow: place one box on the middle slice/frame, propagate bidirectionally via the memory bank. Fine-tuned on >455K 3D image–mask pairs + >76K video frames (incl. 251,550 echocardiogram frames); human-in-the-loop labeling cut annotation cost >85%.

Benchmarks: DSC 88.84% (CT organs), 86.68% (CT lesions), 87.06% (MRI organs), 88.37% (MRI lesions), 87.22% (PET lesions), 92.22% on FLARE (beats SAM2.1), 92.24% median DSC on polyp segmentation. Weaker specifically on prostate lesions.

SAM3 (zero-shot 3D medical) ↗

Meta

Meta's next core SAM generation. A dedicated Nov 2025 evaluation, "Comparing SAM 2 and SAM 3 for Zero-Shot Segmentation of 3D Medical Data," tested both across 16 public CT/MRI/ultrasound/endoscopy datasets, 54 anatomical structures. SAM3 is consistently stronger under click prompts — fewer over-segmentation failures, slower prediction-retention decay across slices — though the gap narrows under box/mask prompts on some CT/MR structures.

Practical takeaway: any "SAM2 vs micro-SAM" framing is already one Meta generation behind — SAM3 is the more relevant current baseline for zero-shot volumetric propagation.

SAM 3D Objects & SAM 3D Body ↗

Reconstruction

Not a segmentation model — flagging this explicitly since the name invites confusion. SAM 3D Objects is single-image 3D reconstruction (geometry + texture + layout, incl. occluded regions): a two-stage Diffusion Transformer (~1.2B-param geometry model + ~600M texture/refinement model), evaluated on a new benchmark SA-3DAO (1,000 artist-made objects) with ≥5:1 human-preference win rate. SAM 3D Body is full-body 3D human mesh recovery using a new parametric rig (Momentum Human Rig), beating PromptHMR/SMPLer-X on 3DPW and EMDB.

Note: older, unrelated academic papers also used the string "SAM3D" for 2D-mask-to-point-cloud projection (2023) — those predate and are unconnected to Meta's Nov 2025 release.

SAM2Point ↗

Zero-shot 3D

The clearest "slice-as-video" trick applied outside medicine: voxelizes 3D input and generates six orthogonal "videos" (two scan directions per axis) from a single 3D point/box/mask prompt, fed into unmodified SAM2 — no fine-tuning, no 2D→3D projection heuristics. Demonstrated zero-shot on Objaverse objects, S3DIS/ScanNet indoor scenes, Semantic3D outdoor scenes, and raw KITTI LiDAR.

SAM2 on 3D CT — Known Failure Modes ↗

Evaluation

Zero-shot SAM2 on abdominal CT: mean DSC 0.821 (liver), 0.862/0.870 (kidneys), 0.891 (spleen) — strong on large, high-contrast organs — but far weaker on pancreas, adrenal glands, and low-contrast brain structures (caudate, thalamus). 🩷 micro-SAM — Segment Anything for Microscopy, In Depth

micro-sam (Archit, Freckmann, Pape et al., Computational Cell Analytics group, Uni Göttingen/EMBL) is the dominant open-source SAM adaptation for bio-microscopy, published in Nature Methods (2024). Unlike SAM2, it does not use a native 3D/video architecture — it wraps SAM's 2D encoder in a domain-finetuning + slice-merging strategy, which is precisely why it is now absorbing SAM2 as a backbone (see the Head-to-Head tab).

🧭 Animated Explorer: micro-SAM's 3D Slice-Merge Pipeline

Unlike SAM2's learned memory attention, micro-SAM's 3D handling is a documented heuristic: segment the middle slice, propagate outward via IoU-based IDs, no learned cross-slice attention.

No learned attention crosses slice boundaries here — consistency comes from mask-overlap heuristics, which is faster to train per-modality but more prone to ID-swap errors on low-overlap slices than SAM2's learned memory bank.

🧩 Architecture & Backbones

Four ViT backbone sizes: vit_h, vit_l, vit_b, and a lightweight vit_t (MobileSAM-distilled). Ships as napari-plugin "annotator" apps: annotator_2d, annotator_3d (slice propagation), and annotator_tracking (integrates Trackastra for automatic cell tracking).

AMG vs AIS: AMG is SAM's original exhaustive point-grid approach — no extra training, but slow. AIS trains an additional lightweight instance-segmentation decoder jointly with finetuning, enabling fast one-shot segmentation without a prompt grid — the recommended default where a finetuned decoder exists.

📚 Training Data

LM generalist: LIVECell, DeepBacs, TissueNet, NeurIPS CellSeg, PlantSeg (root), the Data Science Bowl nucleus set, 8 Cell Tracking Challenge datasets. Covid-IF held out purely for out-of-distribution evaluation.

EM generalist: MitoEM (rat + human) for mitochondria, PlatyEM/Platynereis for nuclei; evaluated OOD on Lucchi (FIB-SEM), MitoLab, UroCell, and VNC.

📊 Benchmarks — What's Actually Verifiable

The Nature Methods paper's full tables sit behind full-text access this research pass couldn't reach (persistent 403s on nature.com/PMC/bioRxiv). Qualitative claims are well-supported by paper-adjacent text; hard numbers below come from a separate, later benchmarking paper by the same authors.

✅ Confirmed hard numbers (2026 follow-up paper, not the original)

"Revisiting foundation models for cell instance segmentation" (arXiv:2603.17845, MIDL 2026, same authors) reports on LIVECell: micro-SAM achieves mSA = 0.415 (AIS) and mSA = 0.437 (AMG/APG).

🔶 Qualitative, well-supported claims (original paper)

"Both specialist and generalist models show clear improvements compared to the default [vanilla SAM] model." AIS performs "on par or better than Cellpose except for TissueNet." The EM generalist "provides comparable quality to MitoNet for automatic mitochondrion segmentation while also enabling interactive segmentation."

A newer benchmark, MitoEM 2.0 (bioRxiv 2025), places micro-SAM alongside nnUNet-BC and MitoNet across 8 EM datasets using Accuracy@50 — reporting "fine-tuning yields clear, consistent improvements" for both MitoNet and micro-SAM, though exact per-dataset table values weren't retrievable in this pass.

⚠️ Known Limitations (GitHub-verified)

  • Heuristic 3D, not learned end-to-end — interactive propagation has a documented IoU-threshold stopping rule (it halts once overlap with the prior slice drops too low), a tacit admission that unchecked propagation drifts; automatic mode instead merges independent per-slice segmentations via overlap/Multicut.
  • Scaling issues on very large volumes — GitHub issue #1076 reports a kernel crash running automatic segmentation on a 9532×9998 image even on a 188GB-RAM / RTX 5090 workstation.
  • AMG is slow; AIS/APG need a trained decoder per modality; per-image inference reportedly still trails Cellpose-SAM's speed despite competitive accuracy (secondary-source claim, not independently verified).
  • Uneven generalization by design — separate LM vs. EM-organelle checkpoints exist because a single cross-modality generalist wasn't feasible; the EM model explicitly doesn't yet support organelles beyond mitochondria/nuclei, and SAM-family models broadly struggle with very small/filament-like objects.
  • Finetuning needs consecutive-ID instance labels; CPU finetuning works (32–64GB RAM) but is slow — GPU VRAM directly trades off against batch size/objects-per-image (e.g. an 8GB GTX 1080Ti can only finetune the decoder/prompt-encoder, not the full model).
  • No shipped SAM2/SAM3 backbone in the core library as of v1.8.4 (verified via PyPI) — see the Head-to-Head tab for what's real vs. an earlier mistaken claim about this.

⚖️ micro-SAM vs. SAM2/SAM3: The Actual Evidence

This is the section most directly answering "how do they compare." Short version: they don't compare — yet, in the sense of a shared benchmark table. Here's every piece of real evidence found, graded by how direct it is, followed by a clearly-labeled expert synthesis.

✅ Corrected finding (verified directly via PyPI + GitHub, July 2026): micro-SAM has not merged a SAM2 backbone — an earlier research pass got this wrong

An initial research pass surfaced what looked like specific GitHub pull requests merging a SAM2 backbone into micro-SAM. On independent verification — fetching micro-SAM's actual PyPI package metadata and its official RELEASE_OVERVIEW.md directly — that claim does not hold up: the latest release (v1.8.4) declares only segment-anything (Meta's original SAM1) as a dependency, with no sam2/sam3 package anywhere in the tree or changelog. That specific PR list was a research-agent hallucination, and it's corrected here rather than left in the report.

What is real and verified: a March 2026 paper by the same authors, "Revisiting foundation models for cell instance segmentation" (arXiv:2603.17845, MIDL 2026), introduces a new automatic segmentation strategy called APG (Automatic Prompt Generation) and directly benchmarks μSAM alongside SAM, SAM2, SAM3, CellPoseSAM, and CellSAM as baselines — on LIVECell, μSAM scores mSA 0.415 (AIS) / 0.437 (AMG/APG). That's a genuine, current comparison-in-spirit; it just doesn't mean the two codebases have merged.

📈 Convergence Timeline

2023–24

micro-SAM (SAM1-based) and SAM2 develop in separate communities — bio-image analysis vs. video/clinical imaging.

2025

Parallel adjacent efforts (SAM4EM, BioSAM2, SAMM) show SAM2's memory helps 3D consistency — but none integrate with micro-SAM's codebase.

2026 (now)

micro-SAM's own authors publish a direct benchmark of μSAM vs SAM2, SAM3, CellPoseSAM & CellSAM (arXiv:2603.17845) — but the shipped library (v1.8.4) is still SAM1-only, verified via PyPI.

Open question

Whether that benchmarking effort turns into an actual SAM2/SAM3-backed release remains to be seen — worth re-checking micro-SAM's changelog periodically.

nnInteractive ↗

Medical

3D-native (not slice-by-slice) interactive segmentation, trained on 120+ heterogeneous 3D datasets (CT/MRI/PET/3D microscopy). Adds a novel lasso prompt alongside points/scribbles/boxes. Client/server split lets a lightweight client drive remote GPU inference.

ENSAM ↗

Medical

SegResNet-style U-Net (encoder + prompt encoder + mask decoder) with latent cross-attention and the Muon optimizer — trained from scratch (no pretrained backbone) on <5,000 volumes across CT/MRI/PET/ultrasound/microscopy in just 6 hours on a single 32GB GPU. A deliberate low-compute counterpoint to giant pretrained foundation models.

Benchmarks: CVPR 2025 challenge hidden test set — DSC AUC 2.404, NSD AUC 2.266; beats VISTA3D and SAM-Med3D baselines, matches SegVol despite far less data/compute.

VoxTell ↗

Medical

Free-text promptable 3D segmentation — no spatial prompt at all. A vision-language model maps text (single words to full clinical sentences) directly to 3D masks via multi-stage vision-language fusion across decoder layers, aligning text and visual features at multiple scales. Benchmarks: SOTA zero-shot on unseen datasets/modalities; strong cross-modality transfer and robustness to phrasing variation.

SynthFM-3D ↗

Medical

Not a new network — a synthetic-data training methodology. An analytical framework mathematically models 3D variability in anatomy shape, contrast, boundary sharpness, and noise to generate purely synthetic 3D training volumes (zero real patient data/annotations), then fine-tunes SAM2 on 10,000 synthetic volumes.

Benchmarks: statistically significant Dice improvements over pretrained SAM2 on 11 anatomical structures across 3 modalities and 5 public datasets — with zero real training data.

DINO-Med3D ↗

Medical

A distinct architectural bet from the whole SAM lineage: repurposes a pretrained DINOv3 ViT (2D self-supervised, not built for volumes) via two-stage progressive adaptation — Stage 1 fine-tunes on 2.5D stacked slices; Stage 2 fuses with a high-res 3D CNN via gated fusion, lifts to 3D feature pyramids, and decodes with a UPerNet-style 3D decoder plus a detail-recovery stream for boundary precision.

Benchmarks: reported to "significantly outperform" SOTA baselines on 5 public datasets (Dice, HD95).

Cellpose-SAM ↗

Microscopy

Adapts SAM's pretrained transformer image encoder into the existing Cellpose framework, replacing its original encoder while keeping Cellpose's flow-field instance decoding. Trained on 22,826 light-microscopy/histopathology images with 3.34M annotated cells and nuclei.

Benchmarks: substantially outperforms inter-human annotator agreement, approaches the human-consensus upper bound; robust to channel shuffling, cell-size variation, shot noise, downsampling, isotropic/anisotropic blur.

3D Light-Sheet Microscopy Foundation Model ↗

Microscopy

A genuinely 3D-native, multimodal foundation model for light-sheet fluorescence microscopy volumes — pretrained via joint masked-volume reconstruction + image-text alignment on a large curated multi-organism, multi-stain, multi-protocol dataset. Produces transferable volumetric representations for few-shot segmentation, classification, and deblurring from one shared backbone.

PointINS ↗

Point Cloud

Addresses a known weakness of point-cloud self-supervised foundation models: representations transfer poorly to instance localization without full fine-tuning. Adds an orthogonal offset branch that jointly learns semantic understanding and instance/geometric reasoning during pretraining — instance-awareness "for free."

Benchmarks: +3.5% mAP average for indoor instance segmentation, +4.1% PQ for outdoor panoptic segmentation over prior SSL baselines.

OpenVoxel ↗

Point Cloud

Training-free open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding. Operates on sparse-voxel-rasterization reconstructions from multi-view images; instead of embedding CLIP/BERT features per-voxel, groups voxels into object clusters and captions each with a multimodal LLM, then matches text-to-text for open-vocab / referring-expression queries.

Benchmarks: +13.2% over training-based ReferSplat on Ref-LeRF; ~10× faster since no per-scene training is required.
