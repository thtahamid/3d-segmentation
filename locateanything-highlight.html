LocateAnything for EM Segmentation?

How NVIDIA's LocateAnything handles tokenization and bounding boxes internally, whether it is a feasible path for electron-microscopy segmentation the way micro-SAM is used today, and which SAM generation actually powers micro-SAM.

3 questions answered LocateAnything-3B · NVIDIA micro-SAM · SAM1 backbone

⚡ Bottom line up front

  1. LocateAnything does not segment. It is a vision-language grounding & object-detection model. Its only outputs are bounding boxes and points — never pixel masks. So "how it does segmentation internally" has no answer: it localizes, it does not segment.
  2. Not a drop-in for EM segmentation. micro-SAM produces dense instance masks on in-domain microscopy; LocateAnything produces boxes from text descriptions on natural-image / GUI / document data. At most it could act as an out-of-domain box-prompt generator feeding a SAM mask decoder — a role micro-SAM already covers better.
  3. micro-SAM uses SAM 1 — neither SAM2 nor SAM3. Its finetuned encoders are SAM's ViT-T/B/L/H image encoders. SAM2 uses Hiera encoders; SAM3 is Meta's Nov-2025 release. SAM2/SAM3 support is roadmap-only, not the shipped backbone.

Q1 How LocateAnything works internally

Tokenization, bounding boxes, and the reason "segmentation" is a category error for this model.

⚠️ Framing correction

LocateAnything-3B (NVIDIA, arXiv 2605.27365) is a Vision-Language grounding model, marketed as an object detector ("goodbye YOLO"). It answers "where is the thing I described?" with boxes and points. It has no mask head and produces no pixel-level segmentation. Everything below is about how it emits coordinates, not masks.

A Moon-ViT encoder extracts visual tokens at native resolution (preserving fine spatial detail for precise localization), an MLP projector bridges into a Qwen2.5 language decoder, and the decoder emits localization as a sequence of structured blocks. ~3B parameters, trained on ~12M images / 138M queries / 785M boxes across detection, GUI grounding, OCR, document layout and robotics — no microscopy or EM data.

Tokenization — box-aligned atomic blocks

Instead of streaming free-form coordinate tokens, LocateAnything formulates localization as a sequence of fixed-length, "box-aligned" atomic blocks. Four functional block types jointly specify each predicted entity or a termination state:

Semantic

Identifies the entity / class the block refers to.

Box

The full coordinate set (x1,y1,x2,y2) — or a point — treated as one atomic unit.

Negative

Signals the queried object is absent (a true negative).

End

Termination state that ends the prediction sequence.

Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) — the headline trick

Classic VLM grounding decodes coordinates autoregressively, one token at a time, which is slow and can chop a box into arbitrary token chunks. PBD instead treats each box as a constant-length atomic unit and predicts all four coordinates in a single parallel step. This preserves geometric coherence and boosts throughput dramatically.

ModeDecodingTrade-off
Fast (MTP)multi-token, parallelMaximum throughput — predicts full boxes in one step.
Slow (NTP)next-token, autoregressiveMaximum stability — decodes coordinates token-by-token.
Hybridfast → fallbackFast by default; falls back to slow on format irregularity or spatial ambiguity.

🚀 Why it matters

PBD reports >10× faster decoding than Qwen3-VL and ~2.5× faster than Rex-Omni in boxes/second, while improving high-IoU localization quality. The innovation is entirely about fast, coherent coordinate emission — not about denser or pixel-level outputs.

🧩 Why it's a poor direct fit for EM

1. No masks. EM segmentation of organelles, membranes, vesicles and cells needs pixel masks; LocateAnything cannot produce them.

2. Language-grounded. You describe an object in text and it points to it. EM structures are densely packed and near-identical — hard to disambiguate in words. That is the opposite of the sparse, nameable objects grounding models excel at.

3. Zero in-domain data. Its training set contains no microscopy, so zero-shot EM behaviour would be unreliable.

💡 The one narrow role it could play

As a detection front-end in a two-stage detect-then-segment pipeline: a detector proposes boxes, which then prompt a SAM-style mask decoder to produce masks. But this would require heavy EM fine-tuning, and micro-SAM / SAM already provide automatic mask generation and interactive box/point prompting in domain — so the marginal value is low.

🚩

Verdict Not a replacement

LocateAnything is not feasible as a standalone EM segmentation model and is not comparable to how micro-SAM is used today. At best it is an optional, out-of-domain box-prompt generator — and even that role is awkward for dense, hard-to-name EM structures.

Q3 Does micro-SAM use SAM2 or SAM3?

Neither — it is built on the original Segment Anything (SAM 1).

micro-SAM builds on the original SAM (SAM 1), adding a custom instance-segmentation decoder on top of SAM's backbone. The concrete evidence:

🔍 How we know it's SAM 1

micro-SAM's models are named for SAM's ViT image encoders — vit_t, vit_b, vit_l, vit_h, including EM-specific vit_b_em_organelles / vit_l_em_organelles. Those ViT encoders are the SAM 1 architecture. SAM 2 replaced them with Hiera hierarchical encoders (not ViT-B/L/H), so the encoder naming alone pins micro-SAM to SAM 1.

📌 Consistent with this repo's prior deep dive

The companion micro-SAM vs. SAM2/SAM3 report reached the same conclusion: "the micro-SAM library itself still ships only a SAM1 backbone." SAM2/SAM3 appear on the roadmap, but the default generalist and EM-finetuned models remain SAM 1.
